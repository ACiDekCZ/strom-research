# Method: proving a link (parents, marriage)

1. **Premise:** `strom searched B… --years …` and `strom task show T…` — if the
   range was already covered completely, do not repeat it; say so and close.
2. **Index before book.** Use an index to find the page, then read the entry in
   the register itself. An index entry alone is a lead — and so is its absence:
   an index is a copy, written by another hand. A near spelling of the surname
   in the right house or years (one letter off, a similar-looking letter of the
   old script) is a candidate to check in the register by its parents, not a
   different family: ask for that page (`strom task wait`) before you rule it
   out.
3. **Calibrate** the book before counting pages: measure image ↔ page at two
   distant places (`strom recordset calibrate B… --point 95=189 --point 300=611`).
   Never compute a page you have not measured in a book that is not linear.
4. **Identify** the person by year, place, house number, parents and, above
   all, the mother's maiden name. Two people of the same name in one village
   are common.
5. **Cheapest route:** a death or burial entry gives age and often parents; a
   marriage entry names both sets of parents and the ages of the couple; the age
   column is an independent check of a birth year.
6. **Extract the whole entry** (see core). Godparents and witnesses are often
   relatives and prove the next link.
7. **Record** (see "recording an entry"): source (with transcript in the
   original language), facts with citations, names, family links, the search
   (found or negative), lessons about the book. `proven` only for what you read
   yourself and the entry states directly.
8. Done when the entry is found and recorded — or the agreed range was searched
   completely without it (negative search recorded).
